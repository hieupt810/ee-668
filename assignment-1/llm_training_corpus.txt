Title: Foundations of Statistical Language Modeling and Natural Language Processing

Section 1: Introduction to Autoregressive Sequence Modeling
Autoregressive language models formulate the probability distribution of a sequence of tokens as the product of conditional probabilities over subsequent tokens. Given an arbitrary token sequence x = (x_1, x_2, ..., x_T), the joint probability P(x) is factorized via the probabilistic chain rule as the product from t=1 to T of P(x_t | x_1, ..., x_{t-1}). During training, inputs are tokenized into subword units using algorithms such as Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE), WordPiece, or Unigram Language Modeling. Fixed-length context windows, or context lengths denoted by L, define the span of historical dependencies accessible to the attention heads.

Section 2: Transformer Architectures and Multi-Head Self-Attention
The core building block of modern foundation models is the Transformer architecture. Self-attention mechanisms map queries, keys, and values to an output vector space using scaled dot-product attention: Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(Q K^T / sqrt(d_k)) V. Multi-head attention allows the model to jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces at different positions. Positional encodings—ranging from sinusoidal encodings to rotary position embeddings (RoPE) and ALiBi—infuse crucial token order signals into permutation-invariant self-attention matrices. Feed-forward networks (FFN) with SwiGLU or GELU activation functions then project the attended representations non-linearly.

Section 3: Training Dynamics, Loss Formulations, and Optimization
Training large language models involves minimizing the empirical cross-entropy loss over vast corpora. Optimization is predominantly conducted using decoupled weight decay variants of adaptive gradient algorithms, specifically AdamW. Learning rate scheduling typically adheres to a linear warmup phase followed by cosine decay or inverse square root decay. Regularization techniques include weight decay, gradient clipping, dropout (often omitted in modern massive scale architectures to maximize compute efficiency), and architectural normalization via Root Mean Square Normalization (RMSNorm) placed in a pre-normalization schema.

Section 4: Text Sampling, Inference Strategies, and Token Generation
During auto-regressive generation, the model predicts logits over the vocabulary at step t+1. Decoding strategies dictate sequence diversity and coherence. Greedy decoding selects the argmax token, often leading to repetitive loops. Temperature scaling rescales the logit distribution prior to applying the softmax function. Top-k filtering restricts the candidate pool to the k highest-probability tokens, while Top-p (nucleus) sampling dynamically truncates the distribution to include the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability exceeds threshold p. Contrastive search and beam search represent alternative paradigms balancing deterministic exploration with semantic diversity.

Top-k filtering restricts the candidate pool to the k highest-probability tokens, while Top-p (nucleus) sampling dynamically truncates the distribution to include the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability exceeds threshold p. Contrastive search and beam search represent alternative paradigms balancing deterministic exploration with semantic diversity.
